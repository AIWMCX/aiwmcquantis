## 1. Qualification call (15 minutes): checklist
- [ ] **The decision, in one sentence.** For example: "Hire a second technician in January."
- [ ] **Deadline.** When must they decide? No date in the next ~60 days means no urgency, so nurture rather than sell.
- [ ] **Amount at stake.** Salary, purchase price or lease commitment.
- [ ] **The number they're least sure about.** This becomes the focus of the review.
- [ ] **What they'd do with the result.** Decide, take to a lender or partner, or set a stop rule.
- [ ] **Inputs they already have:**
  - 12 months of revenue and costs, or an estimate.
  - Payroll and payment terms.
  - Quotes.
- [ ] **Fit check:**
  - One owner and one decision → **$349**.
  - An advisor with 2 client cases → **$499**.
  - Anything else → say no politely.

## 3. Delivery checklist (do this before sending the memo)
- [ ] Every input traces to something the customer gave you, or is labelled an assumption.
- [ ] Arithmetic is checked by hand for cash, break-even and payback, and matches Quantis.
- [ ] The downside scenario is realistic, not a token 5% haircut.
- [ ] The memo states its limitations and makes no promise of outcome.
- [ ] After the review call, ask: "What did you decide, and what would have made this more useful?" Record the answer in Commercial Ops.
- [ ] Ask for one next step: another decision, an introduction, or permission to quote their feedback by name.

## 4. Avoid double-contacting
When you message a prospect personally, **cancel their queued automated email first.** In Commercial Ops, use the follow-up's cancel action; the sender then skips it. One person should never get your personal note and an automated email in the same week.

## 5. Record keeping (so results are measurable)
Update the stage in Commercial Ops at each step: contacted → replied → call_planned → call_completed → offer_presented → checkout_started → won or lost. The weekly count only works if every step is logged.
