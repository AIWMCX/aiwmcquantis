# Prospect Call & Intake Script — Founding Pilot

**Purpose:** answer Domain 12 Q111–Q117 and Q120 with real evidence instead of guesses.
Every question below produces either a decision, a number, or an observable behavior.
Nothing on this list is answered by looking at code or the database — this is the one part
of the 200-question diagnostic that only real conversations can close.

**Goal for the first 14 days:** 10 qualified conversations, 5 guided pilots, 3 paid
Founding-Pilot buyers. Run this script on every call. Fill in every field. Do not
compress. If a prospect refuses a question, note "declined" — that itself is data.

---

- [ ] **Evidence they handle client decisions at all** (URL to a case study, LinkedIn

If NOT qualified: politely decline the call. Free hour of pilot work does not become
a prospect it wasn't already. Save it for someone who fits.

## Section 2 — On the call: 45–60 minutes, structured

### Opening (2 min) — no pitching yet

> "Thanks for the time. I'm going to spend the first fifteen minutes just
> understanding what your week looks like, then walk you through Quantis on one of
> your real client cases, then we'll decide together whether it's worth another
> conversation. I'll be taking a lot of notes — most useful conversations for me
> right now are the ones that teach me something, not the ones where I get to talk."

Then shut up and let them start. If they ask "what does Quantis do" first, deflect
once: "I'll show you rather than tell you — but first I need to understand what
you're actually solving day-to-day, or the demo won't land."

---

### Q111 — Which persona has a recurring need? (Domain 12)

Ask directly:
1. "How many client-facing decisions did you help evaluate in the last four weeks?"
2. "Of those, how many involved projecting cash, payback, or profitability more than
   six months out?"
3. "Walk me through the most recent one, start to finish."

Record verbatim. Look for: repeat cadence, decision language, whose money is at stake.

**Qualifier for QUALIFIED status:** answer ≥2 to question 2 AND can describe the most
recent one in specific detail (numbers, names, real client). Vague answers = not this
persona, not this month.

---

### Q112 — What event triggered the last case?

1. "For that most recent case — what actually made your client come to you *that week*
   instead of some other week?"
2. "Was there a deadline? A dollar amount they needed to commit? Someone else asking
   for a number?"
3. "If they hadn't come to you, what would they have done instead?"

Record the trigger verbatim. Categorize (write in your notes):
- Capital commitment deadline (lease, hire, equipment, investor answer)
- External request (bank, board, investor, spouse, buyer)
- Internal breakage (missed target, cash shortfall)
- Curiosity / annual planning
- Other: ______________

**Signal:** triggers in the first three buckets are worth pursuing. Triggers in the
last two rarely convert to paid work.

---

### Q113 — Is this decision expensive enough to prepay for the analysis?

Don't ask directly. Instead:
1. "What was the size of the decision in dollars?" (capital at risk, or the swing
   between best- and worst-case outcomes)
2. "How much time did you personally spend on it?"
3. "What did you charge them for it?"

Then compute silently: if the fee they charged is >5× your $149 pilot price AND the
decision size was >$25K, this prospect can pay. If their fee was under $500 or the
decision was under $10K, they can't afford Quantis at any price that covers your time.

---

### Q114 — What are they replacing? (alternative-to-Quantis)

1. "Walk me through how you actually built the numbers on that case. What tool, what
   template, what benchmarks?"
2. "How long did the modeling itself take, separate from the client conversation?"
3. "What did you *want* to know that you couldn't get to?"

Record every specific tool named: Excel template names, ChatGPT prompts, LivePlan,
research subscriptions, prior deals as benchmarks. That is your competitive set.

**Signal:** "I built it in Excel from scratch" or "I asked ChatGPT and cleaned it up
manually" are both strong buy signals — those are exactly the workflows Quantis
compresses. "I use LivePlan / Fathom / Jirav" means you're competing on features, not
time — harder sale.

---

### Q115 — Recurring case volume

Already partially answered by Q111. Confirm with:
1. "In a normal month, how many of these do you do?"
2. "How many *could* you do if you had a faster tool?"
3. "Which client segment gives you the most of these?"

**QUALIFIED threshold (from Domain 12 Q115): ≥2 relevant cases per month.**

---

### Q116 — Buying committee

1. "For someone in your role, who typically decides on new tools like this — you, or
   someone else?"
2. "If we were to work together on a pilot, who would need to sign off?"
3. "Do you bill clients for tools you use, or eat the cost yourself?"

Map the committee: **Decider · Doer · Blocker · Payer.** For solo consultants these
usually collapse to one person; for anyone in a firm, they don't. Sales cycle length
scales directly with this count.

---

### Q117 — Value ranking

Show them 5 cards (physical or virtual) and ask: "Rank these in the order they'd help
you most in the case you just described."

1. **Speed** — get to a defensible answer in an hour instead of a day
2. **Defensibility** — every number traces to a source you can point to when
   challenged
3. **Client confidence** — the report itself signals rigor to your client
4. **Accuracy** — the numbers are more likely to be correct than what you'd build
5. **Breadth** — covers scenarios (best/base/worst) you wouldn't have built alone

Record the ranking verbatim. **Signal:** if speed and client-confidence are 1 & 2,
this is a Founding Pilot buyer. If accuracy is #1 with everything else far behind,
they're going to disagree with the model shape (see Domain 5, Q41) — probably not
a fit yet.

---

### The demo (15 min)

Not a slide deck. Run Quantis live on the actual client case they just described,
with them watching. Follow this order:

1. Idea box: paste one sentence describing their client. Do not oversell — the
   sentence should be exactly what they said.
2. Fill fields with their real numbers (or realistic proxies if they don't have
   them handy). Talk them through the "unsure? leave blank" affordance for the
   ones they don't know.
3. Let it run. Do not narrate. Watch their face.
4. When the report renders, do not explain it. Ask: "What do you notice first?"
5. Then: "Is anything below what you'd have expected?"
6. Then: "Which of these three assumptions, if it changed, would flip your
   recommendation?"

Record their verbatim reactions in every question above. That is the entire product
research pipeline for the next 30 days.

**If the ModelApplicabilityBanner fires** (revenue-shape mismatch — contingency fee,
project-based, transaction percentage — see `revenue-shape-detector.ts`): acknowledge
it explicitly. "The engine flagged that this business's revenue shape doesn't match
its steady-monthly-transactions model. I want you to see when it does that — that's
the honesty layer." Do not try to hide the banner. Prospects reward this behavior.

---

### The ask (5 min)

Only after they've seen the report, and only if the earlier signals say they're
qualified:

> "I'm running founding pilots at $149 for the first three consultants I work with.
> One session like this on a real client case of yours, one reviewed report, one
> assumption revision, one week of follow-up. Would you want to be one of them?"

Three outcomes to record:

- **YES, will pay now** — send Stripe payment link before you hang up. Book the
  follow-up session before you disconnect. This is the finish line.
- **YES, but need time to think** — book the follow-up conversation for a specific
  date. Send a summary email that same day. Do not just email a payment link and
  hope.
- **NO** — ask "what would have to be true for that to make sense?" Record verbatim.
  Do not argue. Do not offer a free session. Thank them and move on. Every recorded
  "no because ___" is more valuable than another warm maybe.

## Section 4 — Q120: Which ICP to REJECT for the next 30 days

Update this list at the end of every week based on what you learned. Founder decision,
not customer decision — but the calls give you the raw material.

Initial rejection list (before any data):

- **Pre-launch solo founders with no capital committed** — they don't have a real
  decision yet; Quantis's shape isn't a good fit for exploratory ideation.
- **In-house corporate finance teams** — they have Excel + a bank; you can't beat
  what they've already built.
- **Contingency-fee / project-based businesses** as SELF-USERS — the engine's
  revenue-shape detector (`revenue-shape-detector.ts`) will fire on their own
  business, undermining trust. They CAN still be buyers for their *clients'*
  cases if the clients are steady-revenue.

Add to this list as you find them. Reject them cleanly and fast.

---

## Section 5 — Success criteria for this script

After 20 completed calls following this template, you should be able to answer, with
evidence:

- Q111: which single persona has the highest paying-per-week rate
- Q112: the top 3 triggers that produced a paid pilot
- Q113: the actual decision-size threshold below which prepayment doesn't happen
- Q114: the #1 named alternative (probably Excel or ChatGPT — but confirm)
- Q115: what "≥2 cases/month" looks like in practice by persona
- Q116: median buying-committee size
- Q117: what buyers say they want first (probably speed) vs. what actually made them
  pay (probably client-confidence)
- Q120: an evidence-backed rejection list of 3–5 personas you will not pursue

At that point Domain 12 stops being UNKNOWN and starts being VERIFIED — the whole
reason the diagnostic left those questions open in the first place.

Do not shorten this script. The prompts above are the difference between building
a business on evidence and building one on the founder's own guess about the customer.
